I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at [Seoul National University](https://www.snu.ac.kr), working with Prof. Hunpyo Lee. I received my Ph.D. from the [Indian Institute of Science (IISc)](https://iisc.ac.in), Bangalore. My research lies at the interface of **condensed matter theory**, **quantum many-body physics**, and **numerical methods**.

## Research Interests

My work spans three interrelated areas of theoretical condensed matter physics:

### Many-Body Localization (MBL)
I study the ergodicity-breaking transition in quantum systems, focusing on how disorder and interactions conspire to localize quantum information. My work includes MBL in systems with random long-range interactions, quasiperiodic potentials (Aubry-André type), and the dynamics of entanglement and single-particle excitations across the MBL transition. I also investigate how the initial state affects dynamical signatures near the MBL phase boundary.

### Strongly Correlated Systems: Bilayer Hubbard Models
I use **Determinant Quantum Monte Carlo (DQMC)** to study correlated electrons in bilayer lattice geometries. My work addresses the finite-temperature phase diagram of the bilayer band insulator-to-Mott insulator crossover, the interplay of inter-layer hopping with disorder and strong interactions, and pairing correlations in the presence of disorder. These systems are relevant for understanding transition metal oxides, twisted bilayer materials, and optical lattice experiments.

### Topological Superconductivity and Quantum Geometry
My recent research explores **proximity-induced topological superconductivity** in heterostructures combining topological insulators or Rashba materials with conventional superconductors. I compute optical conductivity and spectral functions using the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) formalism combined with the Kubo formula, revealing signatures of Majorana edge modes and the role of **quantum geometry** (Berry curvature, quantum metric) in optical response.
